WHO Body Mass Index Standards

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple index of weight-for-height that is commonly used to classify underweight, overweight, and obesity in adults. It is defined as a person's weight in kilograms divided by the square of their height in meters (kg/m²).

Underweight

< 18.5

May indicate malnutrition or underlying medical conditions. Consultation with a nutritionist is recommended.

Normal Weight

18.5 – 24.9

Lowest statistical risk for cardiovascular diseases, type 2 diabetes, and hypertension.

Overweight

25.0 – 29.9

Associated with moderate increase in metabolic risks. Lifestyle modifications are beneficial.

Obese Class I-III

≥ 30.0

Significantly elevated risk for chronic health issues. Structured weight management programs recommended.
